# Silver's Hoard

A local Pokémon battle simulator for the Hoard family. It runs on Pokémon Showdown's battle engine (through `@pkmn/sim`), so every generation from 1 to 9, singles and doubles, plays with the real mechanics. You keep trainers, fight them or watch them fight, build gyms and leagues, and send your trainers and gyms to friends as a page they open in any browser.

## What it does

- **Trainers.** A name, a title, a team, an AI level and style, an avatar and four battle lines (start, last Pokémon, win, lose). Create them by pasting a Showdown export (or PokéPaste, JSON, a packed team), with the visual team builder (species, item, ability, nature, Tera type and moves; each move slot opens a list of what the species learns, with type, category, power, accuracy, PP and the in-game description (in Spanish when the app is), filters and search, and the move can still be typed by hand; training as Pokémon Champions does it, 66 stat points with at most 32 in one stat and each one a stat point more at level 50, stored as EVs, or classic EVs and IVs; the team cards show the points; below it, a damage calculator: each move of a team member against any Pokémon or one from another trainer, with the 16 damage rolls, critical hits and hits to KO, stat boosts, Tera, Mega Evolution, weather, terrain, screens and HP left, measured with the game's own formula), or roll a random one from Showdown's random-battle sets. Duplicate, favourite, trash and restore. *Sets* on each team member shows Smogon's popular sets for the format (or the nearest one with data) and the species' usage on Showdown's ladder (items, abilities, moves, Tera types, teammates); one click fills the member. *Sample team* builds a team from the format's most used Pokémon. They come from data.pkmn.cc when asked for and are kept a week.
- **Characters of every generation.** 214 Gym Leaders, Elite Four members, champions, rivals, villains and their admins, Frontier Brains and other battle facility bosses from Red and Blue to Scarlet and Violet (and the Stadium games and Legends: Arceus), with the 1,821 teams they used game by game and battle by battle: first battle, rematches, tournaments, battle facilities (the remakes' Battle Tower Master Class, Battle Frontier symbols and prints, Subway, Maison, Tree and Tower pools), multi battles (only their own side), rivals' teams for each starter. Browse them by game: each game is a card that opens its characters in sections (Gym Leaders, Elite Four, champions, captains and kahunas, rivals, villains, battle facilities, tournaments, others), or list them by character with filters for generation, game and category. Each team has its levels, moves, held items, abilities and natures, where it is fought and the game's note, checked against that generation's Pokédex. Open a character to pick its look among all its Showdown sprites (Red and Blue, Yellow, Gold/Silver/Crystal, Ruby/Sapphire, Let's Go, Masters EX…), pick a team, and add it as a trainer, or start an own team for it from that team or from nothing.
- **In-game leagues.** The exact teams of Crystal (Johto and Kanto, Red on Mt. Silver included), FireRed/LeafGreen and Emerald, read from the games' code, and their whole leagues: eight gyms with their badges and types, the Elite Four and the champion, each gym capped at its leader's level. Eleven more leagues are built from the characters' teams of each game: Red/Blue, HeartGold/SoulSilver, Omega Ruby/Alpha Sapphire, Platinum, Black/White, Black 2/White 2, X/Y, Sun/Moon and Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon (Elite Four and champion), Sword (the gyms, the Champion Cup and Leon) and Scarlet/Violet, with their cities, badges and types.
- **Battles.** You against the AI, two people on one computer, or two AIs (played in the background, then replayed). The battle screen shows both sides with their sprites, HP, status, boosts, Terastal, weather, terrain and hazards; a log in Spanish or English (Spanish move, ability and item names, and the battle simulator's own messages in Spanish of Spain for the effects of moves, abilities and items, the field, the weather and why a Pokémon can't move); controls for moves (with type, power, PP and Terastal / Mega / Z / Dynamax when the generation has them), targets in doubles, switches, team preview and forfeit. Every move animates on the turn-based screen too. By default it plays Pokémon Showdown's own animation of that move (the 934 animations of its battle client: Ice Beam's line of icicles, Thunderbolt's bolt from the sky, Body Slam's leap onto the target, Earthquake shaking the field, the backdrop going dark for Hyper Beam…), with the sprites moving as they do there and the effect pictures downloaded once into `data/cache/fx` (the `fx_cache` tool can fetch them all ahead for offline battles); the field shows one of Showdown's backdrops for the format's generation, with its weather and terrain overlays. Settings can switch to Silver's own effects, which also cover any move Showdown has no animation for, and to Silver's gradient field. Silver's effects have the look they have in real time: beams, projectiles, claws, bites and punches, lightning and rocks from the sky, quakes that shake the screen, hit bursts by type with a flash on a critical hit and extra weight on a super effective one, auras for boosts and heals, status particles and weather particles; the damage shows when the move lands, in singles and doubles, in live battles, replays and the playable page. Settings has a switch for it (also on the arena); it follows the system's reduced-motion preference and is skipped at the fastest replay speed. Finished battles replay with play, pause, step, jump to a turn and four speeds. Live battles survive a restart. Battles have sound, in both modes: hits (normal, super effective, not very effective, critical), misses, faints, stat changes, status, healing, switches, a whoosh for each move tinted by its type, click sounds and victory and defeat jingles are synthesised in the browser (no audio files); each Pokémon cries when it is sent out (lower and shorter when it faints), and a battle track picked from the battle and the format's generation loops and fades out at the end. Cries and music are downloaded once and kept in `data/cache/audio`; without internet the battle plays with effects only. A speaker button on the field and a Sound section in Settings set master, music, effects and cries volumes and mute; the playable page has the effects too. Under the arena, *Why* shows each AI decision of the turn on screen: the options it weighed with the expected damage and KOs, the threat it was under, who was faster and, for the expert AI, the candidate turns it played out (for the master, the futures it searched, with each option's share of the search and estimated win); in a live battle it also tells you what the expert AI would do in your place (`battle_explain`). A finished battle becomes a video clip with its sound (*Video*, `battle_video`): a windowless Chrome or Edge plays the replay alone on a 16:9 stage, records the tab's picture and the page's effects, cries and music, and leaves an MP4 in the exports folder, optionally handed to Lumiere's Hoard, the family's video editor (needs `requirements-video.txt`).
- **Real time.** A second way to play every battle, after Pokémon Legends: Z-A: the same trainers and teams on a 2D arena seen from above, with no turns and no PP. Each move has a cooldown in seconds (that game's own value for the 337 moves it has; the rest worked out from PP, power and kind, within 1.3 s of its table on average) that the user's Speed shortens, a wind-up during which the user stands still, and a shape on the field: strikes, dashes, projectiles, beams, cones, areas and blasts that land where the target stood. There is no accuracy: a move hits if it reaches, so you dodge by moving or rolling. Damage, types, abilities and items keep the turn-based formula; stat changes are one stage and last 20 s; sleep becomes drowsiness (cooldowns at half speed); three switches per trainer, back after every knockout; a time limit decides on Pokémon standing, then HP. Holding the lock-on keeps the Pokémon facing the foe, so it strafes and its moves aim at the foe; without it the Pokémon faces where it walks and its moves go that way (snapping to the foe only within 30°), so shots and beams can miss and strikes lunge forward and land only if they reach. The arena is open or, by default for new battles, has 3 to 6 rocks placed from the battle's seed and mirrored for both sides: they block walking, rolls, dashes, projectiles and beams, while quakes and blasts from the sky go over them, and the AI walks around them. Each trainer has a three-segment Mega Power gauge that fills a segment every 8 s (15 s in battles stored with rules version 2; faster when dealing damage): a full segment pays for a Plus move (×1.2 damage, ×1.3 if super effective, secondary effects ×1.67, a third more healing, 20% larger areas, a quarter of the damage through Protect) and a full gauge Mega Evolves a Pokémon holding its Mega Stone (or Rayquaza with Dragon Ascent) once per battle: the Mega forme's stats, types, ability and sprite, every move a Plus move while the gauge drains a segment every 8 s, then back to its usual forme. In doubles formats each trainer has two Pokémon on the field at once: you move one of them (Tab or C swaps which, L on a gamepad) and the AI drives its partner, which stays within reach, dodges, keeps out of the way of your quakes and attacks the foe you lock on (X or the right stick click switches it) unless the other is clearly better; the screen marks the one you move and the foe you lock on, and shows both Pokémon of each side with their slot. Spread moves (Earthquake, Surf, Discharge, Rock Slide, Heat Wave, Hyper Voice...) hit every Pokémon in their area, your partner too for those that hit everything around the user, at ×0.75 when more than one is hit; Helping Hand powers up the partner's next attack ×1.5 and Follow Me and Rage Powder draw the foes' attacks for 3 s, bending projectiles already in the air; each fallen Pokémon's slot is refilled from the bench (you pick from the party bar), the three switches and the gauge are the trainer's, and a trainer loses when nobody is left to come out. The AI trainers play their two Pokémon the same way, choosing targets by damage and threat. Battles stored before these rules keep the old ones (always locked on, open arena, no gauge; doubles formats as singles), so their replays stay exact. You play with the keyboard or a gamepad, laid out as in Legends: Z-A (hold Shift / ZL to lock on, the four moves in a diamond like X A B Y on I L K J or the face buttons, Space / Y to roll, F / ZL + + to arm a Plus move, M / R to Mega Evolve, Q / E and the D-pad to pick a Pokémon and R / ▲ to send it out; a simple layout keeps A B X Y for the moves, is always locked on, and uses L and − for Mega and Plus) against the AI (the same five levels and styles, now with reflexes: dodging, spacing, switching), two people share one keyboard, or two AIs fight and you watch. Battles replay exactly from their seed and inputs, in the app and in the engine, which is how the result of a played battle is checked. Gym and league challenges, series and tournaments can be played in real time too (and friends can fight your gyms and trainers in real time on the playable page), and each trainer's page shows its moves' cooldowns. The arena draws Mystery Dungeon-style walking sprites in eight directions, downloaded once from PMDCollab's SpriteCollab, and each move has its own look: continuous beams (crystals along Ice Beam, zigzag lightning, flame and water jets, psychic rings, dragon helixes, Hyper Beam), shaped projectiles (fireballs, ice shards, leaves, rocks, shadow orbs, stars, crescents), claw marks, punches and bites, lightning and rocks from the sky, quakes that crack the ground and shake the screen, hit bursts by type, auras for boosts and heals, status particles and weather.
- **The AI.** Six levels: random, easy, normal, hard (the best calculated option every turn: damage measured with the game's own formula on a copy of the battle, status, boosts, hazards, recovery, switching, Terastal), expert (it plays each candidate turn out on copies of the battle against the opponent's likely answers and keeps the one with the best outcome; in doubles it does the same with pairings of both Pokémon's best options) and master (it searches hundreds of thousands of possible futures of the turn, about a second a turn, with the Monte Carlo tree search of poke-engine, an optional Rust battle engine: `requirements-master.txt`, `ai_levels`; 9th-generation singles, and elsewhere or without poke-engine it plays as the expert). Five styles: balanced, aggressive, defensive, setup, stall.
- **Series and tournaments.** The same two trainers N times (win rate, average turns, KOs, damage and survival of every Pokémon, the MVP of each side, one sample battle of each outcome), or a tournament between up to 64 trainers: single elimination with byes or a round robin with a table, best of 1, 3, 5 or 7. They run as background jobs.
- **Equalized levels.** Every battle, challenge, series and tournament can put all Pokémon at level 50 or 100 for that fight, so a level 88 team does not crush a level 44 one unless you want it (also a default in Settings).
- **Gyms and leagues.** A gym has gym trainers (fought in order) and a leader, a type, a badge with its colour and picture (one of 18 drawn shapes, a picture of your own, or badges drawn by Prospero's Hoard from a description; the picture travels with the gym and the badges won keep it) and rules (fixed level, level cap, minimum level, extra Showdown rules). Challenge it yourself or let your trainer's AI do it; between battles your team is healed; winning every battle gives the badge. A league needs its badges, then the Elite Four and the champion; whoever beats it enters the hall of fame. A challenge can use the gym's own generation or current rules.
- **Friends.** Export a trainer, gym or league as a file (`.hoardtrainer`, `.hoardgym`, `.hoardleague`) for another Silver's Hoard, a share code to paste in a chat (with your own sprite inside, big drawings shrunk), the team as Showdown text, or a **playable page**: one HTML file of about 2 MB with the battle engine and the AI inside (packed; it unpacks itself when opened). Your friend opens it in any current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari from 2023 on), pastes or rolls a team and fights your gym or trainer, turn by turn or **in real time** (the export asks "Mode for your friend": turn by turn, real time, or their choice for each battle, the default; `friend_mode` on the `export` route and tool). In real time the page runs the same arena, rules, keyboard and gamepad controls, lock-on, rocks, Plus moves and Mega Evolution as the app, with the effects offline and no cries, music or walking sprites (the static battle sprites draw the Pokémon). At the end the page gives a result code (and a result file with the replay). A result with real-time battles (`SILVERWIN2-`) carries, for each one, the rules version, seed, arena, time limit, a key of the opponent's team and the input log (packed: about 4-6 characters per input change, so a keyboard battle of a few minutes is 2,000-6,000 characters and a stick can be several times that: past 6,000 the page pushes "download the code as a file", and the app takes the text or the file). Importing it here replays every real-time battle with the same simulator against the opponent as this app has it, and the battle, the badge or the win are only recorded if the replay ends with the friend's win; an edited code, a changed gym or an illegal team is refused with a message. Turn-based results (`SILVERWIN1-`) work as before. Teams also travel as pokepast.es links: *pokepast.es link* publishes a trainer's team (public, after a confirmation) and Import takes a link back as a new trainer, with the paste's title and the format named in its notes.
- **Battle facilities.** Win streaks as in the Battle Tower: the Emerald and Sinnoh Battle Towers, the Battle Subway (single and double trains), the Battle Maison and the Battle Tree (singles and doubles) and the Galar Battle Tower. One rival after another at level 50, three Pokémon a side (four in doubles) picked at team preview, rivals that get harder each round of seven, and each facility's bosses on their battle with their teams from the games (Palmer on 21 and 49, Ingo and Emmet on 21 and 49, the Battle Chatelaines on 20 and 50, Red and Blue on 20 and 50, Anabel on 35 and 70, Leon every 10; the Battle Tree's legends show up now and then). Play every battle, or let the AI play the streak up to a limit; turn-based or in real time. The record is each trainer's longest streak. The Battle Factory (Emerald, Platinum, and one with Scarlet/Violet sets) lends the team: three of six rental Pokémon each round of seven, and after each win one of them can be swapped for one of the beaten rival's.
- **Progress.** Each trainer's record (by kind of battle), current and best streak, KOs, star Pokémon, facility records, badges, 22 achievements and a Pokédex of the species it used, saw and knocked out, all worked out from what is stored; and every own trainer ranked by wins.
- **Avatars.** Pick any of the 1,500 Showdown trainer sprites, upload a picture, turn a photo into a 96-px pixel-art sprite here (background removed, few colours, outline), or have Prospero's Hoard draw the trainer: from up to four photos of the same person and/or a description, in the games' anime art style taken from style references (three Black and White trainer sprites by default; add your own pictures, such as official art or anime stills, in Settings), cut out with background matting and returned both as the drawing and as the 96-px sprite (a job; Prospero must be running). Prospero can then draw your own sprite again from behind, shown on your side of a battle, and in a victory pose, shown when it wins (Poses tab; they belong to that sprite).
- **Battle lines with AI.** The family's language model (through Hoard Hub: the Sparks, Faustus or Ollama) writes a trainer's four lines from its team and a personality you describe.
- **Family.** Faustus's copilot on every page, an MCP server with 80 tools (`mcp_server.py`), events on the family bus, and the shared Hoard look.

## What it does not do

- No online battles between two copies of the app: friends play your gyms and trainers against the AI in the playable page and send the result back.
- No Pokémon art in the repository or the app: sprites are downloaded from the Showdown sprite server the first time they are needed and kept in `data/cache/sprites`. Without internet the field shows initials until they are cached. Cries and battle music work the same way (`data/cache/audio`).
- The AI knows both teams, as in-game trainers do, but never the move a person has just picked: the expert guesses it. The expert searches in singles and doubles; in triples it plays as hard. The master searches only in 9th-generation singles.
- Real time plays singles and doubles formats (triples play as singles) and leaves out Z-Moves, Dynamax, Terastallization and the Alpha Pokémon of Legends: Z-A; Trick Room, Transform and a few copying moves have no effect there, and in doubles Wide Guard, Quick Guard, Ally Switch and the other ally-targeting moves except Helping Hand, Follow Me and Rage Powder have none either (`rt_rules`, `rt_cooldowns`). On the friends' playable pages it works offline except for the walking sprites, cries and music, which come from the app.
- The result codes are not signed: turn-based ones are a record between friends, not proof; real-time ones are checked by replaying the battle, which catches an edited code but not a battle played by a script or a tool that feeds the simulator inputs.
- Noland and Thorton (Factory Heads) and Argenta (Hall Matron) have no fixed team in their games (rentals, or one Pokémon per type), so they are not among the characters; the Battle Factory facility plays the rental format.
- Planned work is in [OBJETIVOS.md](OBJETIVOS.md) and what is left to check in [PENDIENTES.md](PENDIENTES.md).
- The exact, code-read teams and ready-made leagues cover Crystal, FireRed/LeafGreen and Emerald; the characters of the other games come from Bulbapedia's tables, so their IVs and EVs are not known (they use 31 IVs and no EVs). Battles use held items only: no potions or other bag items. Battle facility pools (Battle Tree, Battle Tower) are left out.

## Install and run

```bash
npm ci                       # Node.js 20 or newer: the engine and the client
npm run build                # the UI and the playable page template
python -m venv venv && venv/Scripts/pip install -r requirements.txt   # or venv/bin/pip on Linux and macOS
venv/Scripts/python -m silvers_hoard    # http://127.0.0.1:5230
```

Hoard Hub finds it through `faustus-plugin.json`. Data lives in `data/` (or `SILVER_DATA_DIR`): `silver.db`, avatars, exports and the sprite cache.

## For assistants

`python mcp_server.py` is a stdio MCP bridge to the running app (it starts it when needed). The tools cover the whole app: `battle_start` / `battle_choose` to play, `series_start` and `tournament_start`, `challenge_start`, `canon_install`, `characters_list` / `character_get` / `character_install`, `stat_points`, `damage_calc`, `facilities_list` (with `challenge_start` kind `facility`), `factory_pick` / `factory_swap`, `progress_get` / `progress_list`, `badge_image_set` / `badge_draw`, `export` / `import`, `avatar_from_photo`, `trainer_dialogue_write`, the real-time tools (`mode: realtime` on `battle_start`, `challenge_start`, `series_start` and `tournament_start`; `rt_cooldowns`, `rt_rules`, `battle_rt_finish`) and the rest (`GET /api/agent/tools`).
