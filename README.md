# Silver's Hoard

A local Pokémon battle simulator for the Hoard family. It runs on Pokémon Showdown's battle engine (through `@pkmn/sim`), so every generation from 1 to 9, singles and doubles, plays with the real mechanics. You keep trainers, fight them or watch them fight, build gyms and leagues, and send your trainers and gyms to friends as a page they open in any browser.

## What it does

- **Trainers.** A name, a title, a team, an AI level and style, an avatar and four battle lines (start, last Pokémon, win, lose). Create them by pasting a Showdown export (or PokéPaste, JSON, a packed team), with the visual team builder (species, item, ability, nature, Tera type and moves; each move slot opens a list of what the species learns, with type, category, power, accuracy, PP and the in-game description (in Spanish when the app is), filters and search, and the move can still be typed by hand; training as Pokémon Champions does it, 66 stat points with at most 32 in one stat and each one a stat point more at level 50, stored as EVs, or classic EVs and IVs; the team cards show the points; below it, a damage calculator: each move of a team member against any Pokémon or one from another trainer, with the 16 damage rolls, critical hits and hits to KO, stat boosts, Tera, Mega Evolution, weather, terrain, screens and HP left, measured with the game's own formula), or roll a random one from Showdown's random-battle sets. Duplicate, favourite, trash and restore.
- **Characters of every generation.** 209 Gym Leaders, Elite Four members, champions, rivals, villains and their admins, Frontier Brains and other battle facility bosses from Red and Blue to Scarlet and Violet (and the Stadium games), with the 1,733 teams they used game by game and battle by battle: first battle, rematches, tournaments, battle facilities (Battle Frontier symbols and prints, Subway, Maison, Tree and Tower pools), multi battles (only their own side), rivals' teams for each starter. Browse them by game: each game is a card that opens its characters in sections (Gym Leaders, Elite Four, champions, captains and kahunas, rivals, villains, battle facilities, tournaments, others), or list them by character with filters for generation, game and category. Each team has its levels, moves, held items, abilities and natures, where it is fought and the game's note, checked against that generation's Pokédex. Open a character to pick its look among all its Showdown sprites (Red and Blue, Yellow, Gold/Silver/Crystal, Ruby/Sapphire, Let's Go, Masters EX…), pick a team, and add it as a trainer, or start an own team for it from that team or from nothing.
- **In-game leagues.** The exact teams of Crystal (Johto and Kanto, Red on Mt. Silver included), FireRed/LeafGreen and Emerald, read from the games' code, and their whole leagues: eight gyms with their badges and types, the Elite Four and the champion, each gym capped at its leader's level. Eleven more leagues are built from the characters' teams of each game: Red/Blue, HeartGold/SoulSilver, Omega Ruby/Alpha Sapphire, Platinum, Black/White, Black 2/White 2, X/Y, Sun/Moon and Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon (Elite Four and champion), Sword (the gyms, the Champion Cup and Leon) and Scarlet/Violet, with their cities, badges and types.
- **Battles.** You against the AI, two people on one computer, or two AIs (played in the background, then replayed). The battle screen shows both sides with their sprites, HP, status, boosts, Terastal, weather, terrain and hazards; a log in Spanish or English (Spanish move, ability and item names); controls for moves (with type, power, PP and Terastal / Mega / Z / Dynamax when the generation has them), targets in doubles, switches, team preview and forfeit. Finished battles replay with play, pause, step, jump to a turn and four speeds. Live battles survive a restart.
- **Real time.** A second way to play every battle, after Pokémon Legends: Z-A: the same trainers and teams on a 2D arena seen from above, with no turns and no PP. Each move has a cooldown in seconds (that game's own value for the 337 moves it has; the rest worked out from PP, power and kind, within 1.3 s of its table on average) that the user's Speed shortens, a wind-up during which the user stands still, and a shape on the field: strikes, dashes, projectiles, beams, cones, areas and blasts that land where the target stood. There is no accuracy: a move hits if it reaches, so you dodge by moving or rolling. Damage, types, abilities and items keep the turn-based formula; stat changes are one stage and last 20 s; sleep becomes drowsiness (cooldowns at half speed); three switches per trainer, back after every knockout; a time limit decides on Pokémon standing, then HP. You play with the keyboard or a gamepad, laid out as in Legends: Z-A (hold Shift / ZL to lock on, the four moves in a diamond like X A B Y on I L K J or the face buttons, Space / Y to roll, Q / E and the D-pad to pick a Pokémon and R / ▲ to send it out; a simple layout keeps A B X Y for the moves) against the AI (the same five levels and styles, now with reflexes: dodging, spacing, switching), two people share one keyboard, or two AIs fight and you watch. Battles replay exactly from their seed and inputs, in the app and in the engine, which is how the result of a played battle is checked. Gym and league challenges, series and tournaments can be played in real time too, and each trainer's page shows its moves' cooldowns. The arena draws Mystery Dungeon-style walking sprites in eight directions, downloaded once from PMDCollab's SpriteCollab, and each move has its own look: continuous beams (crystals along Ice Beam, zigzag lightning, flame and water jets, psychic rings, dragon helixes, Hyper Beam), shaped projectiles (fireballs, ice shards, leaves, rocks, shadow orbs, stars, crescents), claw marks, punches and bites, lightning and rocks from the sky, quakes that crack the ground and shake the screen, hit bursts by type, auras for boosts and heals, status particles and weather.
- **The AI.** Five levels: random, easy, normal, hard (the best calculated option every turn: damage measured with the game's own formula on a copy of the battle, status, boosts, hazards, recovery, switching, Terastal) and expert (it plays each candidate turn out on copies of the battle against the opponent's likely answers and keeps the one with the best outcome; in doubles it does the same with pairings of both Pokémon's best options). Five styles: balanced, aggressive, defensive, setup, stall.
- **Series and tournaments.** The same two trainers N times (win rate, average turns, KOs, damage and survival of every Pokémon, the MVP of each side, one sample battle of each outcome), or a tournament between up to 64 trainers: single elimination with byes or a round robin with a table, best of 1, 3, 5 or 7. They run as background jobs.
- **Equalized levels.** Every battle, challenge, series and tournament can put all Pokémon at level 50 or 100 for that fight, so a level 88 team does not crush a level 44 one unless you want it (also a default in Settings).
- **Gyms and leagues.** A gym has gym trainers (fought in order) and a leader, a type, a badge with its colour and picture (one of 18 drawn shapes, a picture of your own, or badges drawn by Prospero's Hoard from a description; the picture travels with the gym and the badges won keep it) and rules (fixed level, level cap, minimum level, extra Showdown rules). Challenge it yourself or let your trainer's AI do it; between battles your team is healed; winning every battle gives the badge. A league needs its badges, then the Elite Four and the champion; whoever beats it enters the hall of fame. A challenge can use the gym's own generation or current rules.
- **Battle facilities.** Win streaks as in the Battle Tower: the Emerald and Sinnoh Battle Towers, the Battle Subway (single and double trains), the Battle Maison and the Battle Tree (singles and doubles) and the Galar Battle Tower. One rival after another at level 50, three Pokémon a side (four in doubles) picked at team preview, rivals that get harder each round of seven, and each facility's bosses on their battle with their teams from the games (Palmer on 21 and 49, Ingo and Emmet on 21 and 49, the Battle Chatelaines on 20 and 50, Red and Blue on 20 and 50, Anabel on 35 and 70, Leon every 10; the Battle Tree's legends show up now and then). Play every battle, or let the AI play the streak up to a limit; turn-based or in real time. The record is each trainer's longest streak. The Battle Factory (Emerald, Platinum, and one with Scarlet/Violet sets) lends the team: three of six rental Pokémon each round of seven, and after each win one of them can be swapped for one of the beaten rival's.
- **Friends.** Export a trainer, gym or league as a file (`.hoardtrainer`, `.hoardgym`, `.hoardleague`) for another Silver's Hoard, a share code to paste in a chat (with your own sprite inside, big drawings shrunk), the team as Showdown text, or a **playable page**: one HTML file with the battle engine and the AI inside. Your friend opens it in any browser, pastes or rolls a team and fights your gym or trainer; at the end the page gives a result code (and a result file with the replay). Importing it here records their battle and, when they won, their badge.
- **Avatars.** Pick any of the 1,500 Showdown trainer sprites, upload a picture, turn a photo into a 96-px pixel-art sprite here (background removed, few colours, outline), or have Prospero's Hoard draw the trainer: from up to four photos of the same person and/or a description, in the games' anime art style taken from style references (three Black and White trainer sprites by default; add your own pictures, such as official art or anime stills, in Settings), cut out with background matting and returned both as the drawing and as the 96-px sprite (a job; Prospero must be running).
- **Battle lines with AI.** The family's language model (through Hoard Hub: the Sparks, Faustus or Ollama) writes a trainer's four lines from its team and a personality you describe.
- **Family.** Faustus's copilot on every page, an MCP server with 65 tools (`mcp_server.py`), events on the family bus, and the shared Hoard look.

## What it does not do

- No online battles between two copies of the app: friends play your gyms and trainers against the AI in the playable page and send the result back.
- No Pokémon art in the repository or the app: sprites are downloaded from the Showdown sprite server the first time they are needed and kept in `data/cache/sprites`. Without internet the field shows initials until they are cached.
- The AI knows both teams, as in-game trainers do, but never the move a person has just picked: the expert guesses it. The expert searches in singles and doubles; in triples it plays as hard.
- Real time is one on one (doubles formats play as singles) and leaves out Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, Dynamax, Terastallization and the Plus moves of Legends: Z-A; Trick Room, Transform and a few copying moves have no effect there (`rt_rules`, `rt_cooldowns`). Friends' playable pages are turn-based only.
- The result codes are not signed: they are a record between friends, not proof.
- Planned work is in [OBJETIVOS.md](OBJETIVOS.md) and what is left to check in [PENDIENTES.md](PENDIENTES.md).
- The exact, code-read teams and ready-made leagues cover Crystal, FireRed/LeafGreen and Emerald; the characters of the other games come from Bulbapedia's tables, so their IVs and EVs are not known (they use 31 IVs and no EVs). Battles use held items only: no potions or other bag items. Battle facility pools (Battle Tree, Battle Tower) are left out.

## Install and run

```bash
npm ci                       # Node.js 20 or newer: the engine and the client
npm run build                # the UI and the playable page template
python -m venv venv && venv/Scripts/pip install -r requirements.txt   # or venv/bin/pip on Linux and macOS
venv/Scripts/python -m silvers_hoard    # http://127.0.0.1:5230
```

Hoard Hub finds it through `faustus-plugin.json`. Data lives in `data/` (or `SILVER_DATA_DIR`): `silver.db`, avatars, exports and the sprite cache.

## For assistants

`python mcp_server.py` is a stdio MCP bridge to the running app (it starts it when needed). The tools cover the whole app: `battle_start` / `battle_choose` to play, `series_start` and `tournament_start`, `challenge_start`, `canon_install`, `characters_list` / `character_get` / `character_install`, `stat_points`, `damage_calc`, `facilities_list` (with `challenge_start` kind `facility`), `factory_pick` / `factory_swap`, `badge_image_set` / `badge_draw`, `export` / `import`, `avatar_from_photo`, `trainer_dialogue_write`, the real-time tools (`mode: realtime` on `battle_start`, `challenge_start`, `series_start` and `tournament_start`; `rt_cooldowns`, `rt_rules`, `battle_rt_finish`) and the rest (`GET /api/agent/tools`).
