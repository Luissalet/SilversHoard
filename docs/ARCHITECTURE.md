# Silver's Hoard — architecture

## Pieces

```
browser (React UI, client/)  ──/api/ui/call──►  FastAPI app (silvers_hoard/)  ──JSON lines──►  Node engine (engine/src/server.mjs)
MCP client ──stdio── mcp_server.py ──/api/agent/call──►        │                                   @pkmn/sim + ai.mjs
                                                                ├── SQLite data/silver.db            (live battles in memory)
                                                                ├── jobs: node engine/src/cli.mjs  (series, tournaments, watched battles)
                                                                ├── sprites: Showdown sprite server → data/cache/sprites
                                                                ├── cries and battle music: Showdown audio server → data/cache/audio
                                                                ├── walking sprites (real time): PMDCollab SpriteCollab → data/cache/pmd
                                                                └── family: Hoard Hub (copilot, events, LLM, Prospero)
playable page (static/play/play.html) = React battle screen + real-time screen + engine/src (core, ai, match, realtime) inlined and deflated (scripts/pack_play.mjs), data slot filled on export
real-time screen (client/src/battle/rt, lazy chunk) = engine/src/realtime run in the browser on @pkmn/sim without learnsets or descriptions (engine/scripts/rt_lite.mjs); the app replays its inputs with the same files
real-time result of a playable page (SILVERWIN2- code) --import--> services._import_rt_result --rt_replay (cli.mjs)--> the simulator with the app's own opponent
```

## Engine (`engine/src`, ES modules, also bundled into the playable page)

- `core.mjs` — formats (`buildFormatId` adds Showdown custom rules after `@@@`), team import/export (`@pkmn/sets`), `completeSets` (fills ability and Tera type), `applyLevelRules` (fixed level, cap, minimum: applied to the sets because Showdown only applies "Adjust Level" in its validator), validation (`TeamValidator`), random teams (`@pkmn/randoms`, seeded), Pokédex search and learnsets, team summaries.
- `points.mjs` — stat points as in Pokémon Champions (66 per Pokémon, 32 per stat): `evFromSp` / `spFromEv` (HOME's rule: 4 EVs the first point, 8 each more), `evsFromPoints` (limits checked), `pointsFromEvs`, `calcStat`. `teamSummary` adds `points`, `points_total` and `stats` at the set's level; engine command `stat_points`.
- `ai.mjs` — `chooseFor(battle, side, {level, style, rng})`. Reads a deep copy of the battle (`freeze` = `JSON.stringify(battle.toJSON())`, `Battle.fromJSON` of a fresh parse; `fromJSON` keeps references into its input, so a copy is never reused). Damage is `battle.actions.getDamage` on the copy (every generation's formula, abilities, items, weather), times accuracy and expected hits, with absorbing abilities handled. Status, setup, hazards, hazard removal, recovery, protection, screens, weather and terrain have their own scores, weighted by style. Switching weighs the best damage given against the best taken. Team preview orders by type matchup. Doubles: per slot with targets, spread moves, ally damage. Expert (singles): up to nine own options × the opponent's three best moves and two best switches, each played out on copies with two seeds, scored by an evaluation (HP, status, boosts, hazards, screens, KO), choosing 0.65 × weighted mean + 0.35 × worst case. Expert (doubles): each slot's three best moves (every target counts) and best switch, paired without sending one bench Pokémon twice or using two gimmicks, the twelve best pairings × the opponent's best pairing and the two with one slot on its second idea, same playouts and score, within 2.5 s. A playout chooses for the opponent first and then for the AI, so a choice a person already sent is replaced by the guess and never read. Revival Blessing's switch request is answered with a fainted Pokémon. With `trace` (an object), `chooseFor` also says why (`fillTrace`, on a copy, after choosing: the same choice and the same random numbers): each slot's ranked options with expected damage, KO and accuracy (a switch's damage taken and dealt), the threat on the slot, who is faster and, for the expert, every candidate turn played out with its mean and worst result.
- `match.mjs` — `Match`: a battle with two seats; AI seats choose in `advance()`, a person's seat waits for `choose()`. `state(view, since)` returns the new lines for a seat (`viewLines` resolves `|split|` blocks), the pending requests annotated for screens (move type, power, category, description; team sprite ids, types, item and ability names) and the result. Turn limit → tie. `snapshot()` for resuming. With `spec.trace` (every classic battle the app starts; not series or tournaments) each AI decision leaves a note (`compactTrace`); `takeNotes()` hands each out once, `match_start` / `match_choose` / `match_forfeit` / `match_log` return the new ones and the app appends them to the battle's `summary.ai_notes` (kept out of `battle_get`, read with `battle_explain`); `match_advice` traces what the expert would do for a person's side without choosing.
- `stats.mjs` — `summarize` (KOs, damage, faints per Pokémon from the log), `playOut`, `series`, `tournament` (bracket with byes, or round robin with points).
- `calc.mjs` — `damageCalc({attacker, defender, format, moves, attacker_boosts, defender_boosts, attacker_tera, defender_tera, attacker_mega, defender_mega, weather, terrain, screens, defender_hp})`: both Pokémon on a one-on-one battle of the format's generation (switch-in abilities and their weather happen, then the stated field, boosts, Tera and Mega Evolution win; a defender without moves gets Splash), each move prepared as it is used (`ModifyType` / `ModifyMove`: Pixilate, Weather Ball, Tera Blast; the AI's `estimate` does the same through `usedMove`), and `getDamage` with the randomizer pinned to each of the 16 rolls, with and without a critical hit: rolls, min/max (and % of max HP), crit range, rolls that knock out and hits to KO. Engine command `damage_calc`, tool `damage_calc` (agent_tools.py calls the engine directly), panel `client/src/components/DamageCalc.jsx` at the foot of the team builder.
- `server.mjs` — the line protocol (one JSON request per line, one answer per line, errors as data). `cli.mjs` — one-shot runs with progress on stderr.
- `scripts/build_canon.mjs` — parses pret's trainer data (Emerald and FireRed C sources, Crystal asm) into `silvers_hoard/presets/canon.json`: 79 trainers and 4 leagues; empty move slots get the last four level-up moves from Showdown's learnsets, like the games. `scripts/build_names_es.py` — Spanish names from PokeAPI's CSVs into `client/src/battle/names-es.js`. `scripts/build_characters.mjs` — turns `scripts/characters/bulbapedia_parties.json.gz` (Bulbapedia's `{{Party}}` / `{{Pokémon}}` templates of 155 character pages, read from each page's raw wikitext in a browser, since the site refuses scripted clients) into `silvers_hoard/presets/characters.json.gz`: 215 entries (214 shown), 1,821 teams, with `bulbapedia_parties_2.json.gz` (Frontier Brains, villains' admins, more rivals) and `bulbapedia_facility8.json.gz` (`{{lop/facility8}}` rows of the remakes' Battle Tower Master Class, which give stats instead of natures and EVs: the build finds the nature, EVs and IVs that give exactly those stats at level 50), `bulbapedia_facilities.json.gz` (`{{lop/facility}}` rows: battle facility teams with EVs and natures; pools of more than six keep `pool`). Each team has its game cards (`games`: a team of several games is in each) and a role (`category`: leaders, elite, champions, trials, rivals, villains, facilities, tournaments, others) from its class, its battle labels and the character. Every species, move, item, ability and nature goes through the dex of the team's generation (unknown ones are dropped and counted); levels with modes or ranges keep the normal one; tag battles keep the character's side; identical teams of one game merge their labels; rivals' teams per starter are told apart by the Pokémon only that team has; sentences about bag items (potions and the like) leave the note, since battles use held items only; each team gets the Showdown sprite of its game (`-gen1rb`, `-gen2`, `-lgpe`…); Spanish names and battle labels come from `scripts/characters/names_es.json` and `labels_es.json`.

## App (`silvers_hoard`)

- `engine.py` — starts and talks to the Node process (ids, timeouts, restart after a crash), and runs `cli.mjs` jobs with progress and cancel.
- `store.py` — SQLite (migrations V1-V3; V3 adds `badge_image` to gyms and earned badges): `trainers`, `gyms`, `leagues` (with trash), `battles` (seats with their teams, status live/running/ended/failed, omniscient log, summary, snapshot), `runs` (gym and league challenges and their stages), `badges`, `hall`, `jobs`.
- `services.py` — everything: trainers (create, update, random, duplicate, lines with the family's model), canon install (trainers, gyms with badges and level caps, leagues), characters (`characters_list`, `character_get`, `character_install`: a trainer from one team and one sprite, `canon_key` `char:<key>:<team>:<sprite>` so the same pick returns the same trainer, or an own team with `custom`), battles (play, hot-seat, watched; `since` counts lines of the live log; finishing stores the omniscient log, records wins and advances the challenge), challenges (stages, auto mode, badges, hall of fame, optional modern rules), series and tournaments (jobs; sample battles stored), sharing (files, codes, playable pages, result codes), avatars (pixel art, upload, Prospero job through Hoard Hub), settings.
- `sharing.py` — wrappers `silvers-hoard/<kind>`, codes `SILVER1-` / `SILVERWIN1-` (zlib + base64url, inflated with a cap), `SILVERWIN2-` (a result with real-time battles) and its input log (`encode_rt_inputs` / `decode_rt_inputs`: nine integers per row, ticks as deltas, the axes in thousandths, deflated, checked on decoding), `FRIEND_MODES`, `parse_any` (codes, JSON files, playable pages, Showdown text), the playable page writer (fills the `silver-data` script tag).
- Real time on the friends' pages (`services.py`): `export(..., friend_mode)` (choose / turns / realtime) puts `mode` and `rt` in the page data (`_rt_page_info`: the singles format the arena plays, arena, time limit, rules version, and a key per opponent = fingerprint of its team, AI, that format and the level rules). `import_result` hands any result with a `rt` stage to `_import_rt_result`: it checks the code is coherent (stages in the gym's order, none after a lost one, `won` equal to what the stages say), the friend's team against the gym's rules, and for every real-time stage the opponent's key (a gym edited after the export is a conflict, not a forgery), then builds the spec itself from its own trainer (seed, arena, time limit and rules version come from the code; teams, AI, format and level rules do not) and replays it with `rt_replay`; the replay's winner must equal the stage's `won`. Only then are the real-time battles stored (kind `friend`, mode `realtime`, with the replay so the app can watch it) and the badge or the trainer's record written. Turn-based stages in the same result are trusted as before. A gym page lists its trainers in the gym's own order (`_gym_trainers`).
- `sprites.py` — the sprite cache (download once, remember 404s for a week, fallback to gen 5 pixel art), avatars and the photo → sprite filter (corner flood-fill background removal, crop, premultiplied box downscale, light unsharp mask, 32-colour palette from the visible pixels, outline).
- `sounds.py` — the audio cache, same idea as sprites: Pokémon cries (`GET /api/cries/{id}`: species or form id, a form without its own cry falls back to the base species) and battle music (`GET /api/music/{track}`: a whitelist of 18 tracks), downloaded once into `data/cache/audio`, 404s remembered for a week, only audio accepted, `sound_downloads` / `audio_server` settings, `GET /api/sound/stats`.
- Drawn sprites (`avatar_draw`, a job): up to four reference photos and up to three style pictures (`avatar_style_get`: the user's pictures in `data/style_refs`, then the trainer sprites of `prospero_style_sprites`, enlarged without smoothing on white and put side by side on one sheet; given apart, the model drew the reference line-up or copied one outfit) are imported into a "Silver's Hoard" project of Prospero's Hoard through the hub (`studio_import`, every write with a `reason`; style pictures once per content, their asset ids kept in `data/style_assets.json`). `studio_generate_image` gets them as `reference_asset_ids`, photos first, and a prompt that names them by number ("images 1 to 4" are the person, "image 5 is only a style reference", "exactly one character") plus the style text of the settings (aspect 2:3; `prospero_engine` picks the engine, else Prospero's default, Qwen-Image 2.1). `studio_job` is polled (six missed answers in a row before giving up); the Prospero job id is kept on the Silver job, so a restart cancels the render it leaves behind. Each picture is cut out with `studio_edit_image remove_background` (BiRefNet; the local edge fill when it is missing), read from Prospero by asset id (`/api/assets/{id}/file`: tool results through the hub are capped at 20 KB) and kept twice: the drawing, and the 96-px sprite (box shrink, light unsharp mask, palette from the visible pixels only).
- Sprite poses (`avatar_poses_draw`, a job; own `file:` sprites only, never the games' trainer sprites): the sprite is the reference (on white, a 96-px sprite enlarged without smoothing; the drawing saved with it when there is one), one `studio_generate_image` per pose (back view, victory pose) with the style sheet as image 2 and the settings' style text without "facing the viewer"; results come back as sprites or drawings like the avatar. `data/avatar_poses.json` maps each avatar to its poses, so they follow the sprite and need no schema change; `trainer_view` and every battle seat (`_public_seat`) carry `avatar_poses` {back, win}, `useSeats` turns them into `backUrl` / `winUrl` for the arena.
- Battle videos (`battle_video`, a job; `video_status` says what is installed): Playwright drives the installed Chrome or Edge headless (nothing downloaded) on `#/video/<id>?record=1`, a bare page (no sidebar) with the arena and the log on a 16:9 stage. `window.videoRecord()` takes the tab with `getDisplayMedia({preferCurrentTab})` (`--auto-accept-this-tab-capture`) and the page's own sound from `video/tap.js`, which mirrors every Web Audio connection to the speakers and every `<audio>` element (`captureStream`, at the mixer's music level) into one stream; tab capture's own audio is silent headless. MediaRecorder chunks reach Python through exposed functions; at the end (the jingle and the winner's screen held 3.5 s) ffmpeg makes an H.264/AAC MP4 at 30 fps in `data/exports` (WebM without ffmpeg). Leaving the page before closing the browser avoids a minute-long close while it still records. `lumiere=true` sends the file to Lumiere's Hoard (`media_receive`).
- Badge pictures: `gyms.badge_image` is `shape:<id>` (18 shapes drawn by `client/src/components/badges.jsx`, the same file in the playable page) or `file:<name>` (an upload through `/api/badges/upload` or `badge_image_set`, or a `badge_draw` job: Prospero draws badge pins from a description, cut out and kept up to 256 px). Exports carry `badge_image: {kind: shape|image}`; earned badges copy the picture.
- Share codes carry the trainer's own picture too, shrunk to 48 KB at most (`CODE_PICTURE_BYTES`).
- `progress.py` — `compute(trainer, battles, runs, badges, hall)`: the record and by-kind counts from the battles where the trainer holds a seat (its side's summary: KOs, lost, Pokémon; the other side's fainted ones), streaks in battle order, feats (sweep, one left, doubles, real time), per-species MVP, challenge and facility numbers (best streak, Factory best, bosses beaten), and the 22 achievements as tests over those numbers. `progress_get` / `progress_list` (trainers that are not in-game ones).
- `game_leagues.py` — eleven more leagues as data: per gym the character key and team (variant id) of that game, the city, type and badge in English and Spanish, and the Elite Four and champion. `canon_list` lists them with the Crystal/FRLG/Emerald ones and `canon_install(league_key)` installs them through `character_install` (each member once), gyms capped at the leader's level.
- `facilities.py` — the battle facilities (generation, singles or doubles, Pokémon per side, bosses with their battle number and character variant, the Battle Tree's specials), the era's trainer classes and names. A streak is a run of kind `facility` whose stages grow one battle at a time: `_facility_stage` seeds a `random.Random` with run id and battle number and returns the boss's team (a sample of its pool when it has more), now and then a special rival, or a generated trainer with Showdown random sets of the facility's generation (doubles sets from gen 8); rivals are normal for battles 1-7, hard for 8-14, expert after. `_facility_rules`: the challenger's format (a doubles custom game of its generation for a doubles facility), `Picked Team Size` (with `Team Preview` when the format lacks it) and level 50. A win adds a stage instead of ending the run; `auto` chains until a loss or `stop_at`; one open streak per trainer and facility. Records come from the runs (`facilities_list`). Battle Factory facilities (`rental`): the run's challenger JSON holds `phase` (pick, battle, swap), six `rentals` per round (random sets of the generation, seeded by run and round), the `team` of three and the beaten rival's `offer`; `factory_pick` and `factory_swap` set the team and start the battle, `challenge_next` keeps the team in the swap phase; battles are the generation's custom game at level 50 with no trainer row on p1. Page `#/fabrica/<run>`.
- `agent_tools.py` — the 73 tools; `api/` — routes; `main.py` — the app factory (guard, PWA, SPA, copilot from Hoard Link).

## Client (`client/src`)

- `battle/` — shared by the app and the playable page: `protocol.js` (protocol reducer + sentences in Spanish/English), `player.js` (timed playback, seek, skip), `Arena.jsx` (field, sprites with Web Animations, HP cards, field effects, trainer bubbles, log), `fx/` (move effects on this screen, a lazy chunk, see below), `Controls.jsx` (team preview, moves, targets, gimmicks, switches, forced switches, replay bar), `dex.js` (types, sprite ids, Spanish names, sprite URLs), `sfx.js` (which sound goes with which protocol line or real-time event; fast replays play less; effectiveness lines come before the damage, so they pick the impact sound), `SoundControl.jsx` (speaker button and sliders), `move-types.js` (type of every move, generated by `engine/scripts/build_move_types.mjs`, loaded on first use to tint move sounds).
- `audio.js` — no React, shared by the app, the real-time screen and the playable page: a mixer (master / music / effects / cries / mute in localStorage `silver-sound`), effects synthesised with WebAudio (nothing to download), cries fetched from `/api/cries/` and decoded once, battle music as a looping `<audio>` with fades from `/api/music/` (`pickTrack`: the same track for the same battle id, from the format's generation). The AudioContext is only created after the first click or key. The playable page has no server: effects only. Tested without a browser in `engine/test/audio.test.mjs`.
- `pages/` — Inicio, Combatir (battle / series / tournament), Combate, Trabajo (series stats, bracket, table), Entrenadores, Entrenador (team cards, team builder, text, AI, lines, battles), Gimnasios / Gimnasio (editor with the badge picture picker, challenge, badge holders), Ligas / Liga, Personajes (games as cards with sections by role, or by character) / Personaje (sprites, teams by game, own team), Historial, Medallas, Compartir, Ajustes.
- `components/TeamBuilder.jsx` — the visual team builder: move slots that open the list of learnable moves (type, category, power, accuracy, PP, Spanish in-game descriptions from `battle/descs-es.js` loaded on demand, filters, typed moves still accepted) and stat points (`engine/src/points.mjs`) or classic EVs/IVs; `components/badges.jsx` — badge shapes and pictures.
- `play/` — the playable page: target card, the friend's team (paste, random, trainer file), validation with the gym's rules, a turns / real-time picker for each battle when the owner left the choice (`data.mode`), the stages one after another, result code and result file. A real-time stage mounts `battle/rt/RtScreen.jsx` with `offline` (no walking sprites, cries or music are asked for: the static sprites or a dot draw the Pokémon) on a spec built from `data.rt` and a fresh seed (`rtcode.js: newSeed`); when it ends the page replays the inputs itself with `rtRun` (the same run the app does on import; what the replay says is the result) and `rtcode.js` packs the log for the code (`packRtInputs`; `unpackRtInputs` for tests). The code is `SILVERWIN2-` when any stage was in real time, and the page offers the file when it is longer than 6,000 characters. Size: the real-time screen adds about 150 KB to the single file because the simulator data was already in it (the turn-based side needs all of it: learnsets for validation, descriptions for the move tooltips). `vite.play.config.js` runs `scripts/pack_play.mjs` after the single-file inlining: the 8.4 MB module script is deflated into a base64 `<script id="silver-code" type="application/octet-stream">` block, and a classic script at the end of `<body>` inflates it with `DecompressionStream("deflate")` (the result codes already need `CompressionStream`) and appends it as an inline module script, so the browser runs exactly the bytes Vite produced; a browser without it gets a message in `#root`. The file is 2.0 MB instead of 8.4 MB (gzip over the wire stays about 1.47 MB); the build fails if the block does not unpack to the original script (`engine/test/pack-play.test.mjs` covers packing and the loader's place). The `silver-data` slot is untouched, so `sharing.playable_page` and the import of a page work as before.

## Move effects on the turn-based screen (`client/src/battle/fx`, `scripts/gen_move_fx.mjs`)

Every move animates on the classic screen with the painter of the real-time mode (`rt/vfx.js`, drawing only), through a thin adapter instead of copied effect code.

- `protocol.js` adds the cues: the `move` cue carries the move id, spread targets and `[still]`; the `-damage` cue carries the move that caused the hit (empty for poison, weather or recoil, with `source` instead), a critical hit and how effective it was (`state.fx` remembers them until the turn ends).
- `fx/move-data.js` is generated (`node scripts/gen_move_fx.mjs`; `--check` and `engine/test/move-fx.test.mjs` fail when it drifts) from the real-time rules' `rtMove`: per move its type and shape (strike, dash, shot, beam, wave, cone, burst, drop, self, field, whole field) so a move looks alike in both modes, without shipping the simulator to the app.
- `fx/plan.js` (pure, tested) turns a move, the positions of the user and targets and a miss flag into a timeline of painter events, projectile flights and hit bursts; `fitTiming` squeezes it into the pause the line gets (about 80% of it) and skips the move's own effect below a 260 ms pause (the 4x speed), keeping the hit burst.
- `fx/stage.js` (`FxStage`, loaded with a dynamic import by `Arena.jsx`, inlined into the single file of the playable page) lays two canvases over the arena (ground marks under the Pokémon, everything else over them, DPR-aware, no pointer events), reads sprite positions from the DOM (`data-fx-pos` on each sprite: side + slot, so doubles work), runs its own clock, and exposes `cue(anim, {budgetMs, speedMs})`, `sync({weather, mons})` (weather particles and status particles while they last), `clear()` and `destroy()`. A new cue replaces what is still travelling, so effects never pile up. Screen shake moves the arena element a few pixels. A striking move also tells the sprite to lunge at its target (`anim.lunge`).
- `player.js` calls `attachFx(stage)`'s `cue` as it applies each line (never when skipping or seeking, which `clear()` the stage) and holds the line for at least the time the effect needs, so the damage shows after the move lands.
- `fx/setting.js`: the "Animaciones de movimientos" switch (`silver-move-fx` in localStorage through `useStored`, in Settings and on the arena) and `prefers-reduced-motion`, which turns the layer off and leaves the sprites' simple animations.

## Real time (`engine/src/realtime`, `client/src/battle/rt`, `silvers_hoard/pmd.py`)

- `za-data.mjs` — Legends: Z-A's base cooldowns of its 337 moves (Serebii, Bulbapedia), its power changes for fast and multi-hit moves, hits per use, and the Speed rule's constants.
- `moves.mjs` — `rtMove(id, gen)`: a move as real time uses it: base cooldown (Z-A's, else `derivedCooldown` from PP, power and kind, mean error 1.3 s against Z-A's table), `finalCooldown` (3 s off per 100 Speed over 25, floor 3 s, priority 3 s), wind-up ticks, shape (`strike`, `dash`, `shot`, `beam`, `wave`, `cone`, `burst`, `drop`, `self`, `field`), reach and the effects the simulator applies. `cooldownTable` for screens and tools.
- `team.mjs` — format id, generation and sets for real time without core.mjs's random-team generator (keeps the browser chunk smaller).
- Data in the browser: real time reads `Dex` (species, moves, items, abilities, natures, the type chart, `Dex.formats` for the generation and game type) and `toID` from `@pkmn/sim`, and `makeRng` from `ai.mjs` (tree-shaken to that function). `@pkmn/sim`'s `dex.mjs` statically imports every generation's learnsets, the event legality tables, the Pokémon GO data and the English descriptions, which only the team validator and the `desc` / `shortDesc` fields use. `engine/scripts/rt_lite.mjs` lists those modules (`RT_LITE_STUBS`) and `rtLitePlugin` in `vite.config.js` replaces them with empty tables in the app build; the build fails if any chunk other than `CombateRt` bundles `@pkmn/sim`, so no screen that needs learnsets or descriptions gets the stubbed copy. The real-time chunk is 1.97 MB minified, 0.43 MB gzip (6.50 MB and 1.03 MB with the full data). The Node engine and the playable page keep the full `@pkmn/sim`. `engine/test/rt-lite.test.mjs` loads the same stubs in a child process (`fixtures/rt-lite-register.mjs`, Node module hooks) and checks against the full one, through `fixtures/rt-lite-runner.mjs`: every species, move, item, ability, nature and type of generations 1-9 (all fields but the descriptions, in `all()` order, which Metronome draws from), every `rtMove` profile, the type chart, every format's `rtFormatId` / `rtGen`, and AI-vs-AI battles in every generation (singles and doubles, rules versions 1-3, Megas, Metronome) plus the recorded v1 and v2 replays: same summaries, lengths, event digests and screen teams. The move profile has no description field, so Node and the browser build the same profile.
- `sim.mjs` — `RtBattle`: 30 ticks per second, a 28 × 17 m arena, one Pokémon per side (two in doubles, see below). Each tick every seat gives an input `{mx, my, use, dodge, sw, focus, plus, mega}` (`ctl`, `tgt` in doubles) (people from the screen, AI seats from `brain.mjs`, which always focuses); acts are `approach` (walk into range), `windup`, `dash`, `roll` (invulnerable), `recover` (after recharge moves) and `switch`. Damage is the turn-based formula with abilities and items; one-stage timed boosts, timed statuses, residual effects every 4 s, weather, terrain, screens, hazards, protection, substitutes, pivots and forced switches. `view()` is what the screen draws; `summary()` has the classic summary's shape plus `mode`, `seconds` and `reason`; `eventLines` writes the stored English log. `rtRun(spec, {inputs, forfeit, tick})` and `fastForward` replay. Determinism: seeded generators (`makeRng`), fixed ticks, `Math.sqrt` instead of `Math.hypot`, no trigonometry, a person's input applied as the log stores it (rounded); Node and Chrome give identical summaries.
  - Rules versions: `spec.version` 1 (missing) is the first rules; 2 adds free aim, the gauge, Plus moves and Mega Evolution; 3 (what the app stores for new battles) plays doubles formats with two Pokémon per trainer and singles exactly as 2. Input log rows are `[tick, side, mx, my, move, roll, switch, focus, plus, mega]`; seven-column rows from before read as focus 1, plus 0, mega 0. `engine/test/fixtures/rt-legacy-v1.json` holds battles recorded with the first rules and the tests replay them to the same events.
  - Focus: `focus` keeps the Pokémon facing the foe (strafing; moves aim at it; out of range, `approach` first). Without it the facing follows the walking direction; at wind-up the move snaps to the foe within 30° (cos ≥ √3/2), otherwise it goes along the facing: projectiles and beams fly that way, cones and strikes use the facing (a strike lunges 0.6 m), dashes charge that way, drops land at the foe's distance along it.
  - Arena: `spec.arena` `open` (missing) or `rocks`: `placeRocks(seed)` (its own generator, `seed:arena`) gives 3-6 circles mirrored left to right, at least 2.3 m apart, from the walls and from the centre band. `moveBy` pushes Pokémon out of them; projectiles end on them (`fizzle` with `rock`); cones need a clear line; bursts and drops ignore them. `rockOnLine` and `steer` serve the approach walk and the AI.
  - Gauge (version 2): integer units, 3600 per segment, +8 per tick (15 s a segment), +1440 × damage / target's max HP, −15 per tick while Mega Evolved (8 s a segment). `plusFor` spends a segment when a move starts with `plus` (always Plus while Mega). Plus: damage ×1.2 (×1.3 super effective), secondary chances ×1.67 (≤ 100), heals ×4/3, cone and burst reach and drop radius ×1.2, protection pierced for 25% (`protected` with `pierced`); event `plus`. Mega: `prepareRtTeam` adds `mega` (forme, stats, types, ability) for a held Mega Stone or Rayquaza with Dragon Ascent from generation 6; `canMega` needs a full gauge, once per battle; `megaEvolve` swaps the forme (HP in proportion) and runs the ability's entry effects; `megaEnd` reverts when the gauge empties, the Pokémon leaves or faints (events `mega`, `mega_end`).
  - Doubles (rules version 3 and a format whose `gameType` is `doubles`; version 2 plays them as singles): `this.doubles`, `this.units` (the four Pokémon on the field). A trainer (`sides[i]`) owns its party, side conditions, hazards, switches, gauge and Mega Evolution; each of its two slots (`sides[i].units[pos]`, `makeUnit`) has its own position, act, volatile effects, wish and AI memory and reads and writes the trainer's fields through accessors, so the rules written for a side work on a slot. In singles a side is its own single unit (`units = sides`) and every code path is the one version 2 had: the v1 and v2 fixtures (`engine/test/fixtures/rt-legacy-v1.json`, `rt-v2.json`, recorded before doubles existed with `record-v2.mjs`) replay to the same event digests. `foe(unit)` is the foe a slot faces and aims at: a foe using Follow Me / Rage Powder (`lureFor`), else its locked slot (`aim`) if on the field, else the other. `firstFoe` tries the aimed foe then the other for strikes, single-target cones, bursts, drops, dashes and projectiles (which hit the first foe they touch and bend towards a lure in flight); `spreadMove(p)` (`target` allAdjacent / allAdjacentFoes) hits every foe in the area, plus the partner for allAdjacent, through `hitAll` with `SPREAD` ×0.75 when more than one is hit (Life Orb recoil once per move); spread status waves go through every foe they meet. `DOUBLES_MOVES`: Helping Hand (`vol.helpedUntil`, ×1.5 power on the partner's next damaging move, 10 s cooldown), Follow Me and Rage Powder (`vol.followUntil`, 3 s, 12 s cooldown); in singles they stay `noeffect`. Switching per slot: `taken` / `bench` keep the two slots from bringing in the same Pokémon; a fainted slot waits for its replacement (`need`) or stays `vacant` when the bench is empty; `canLeave` replaces the `alive > 1` checks of pivots and Roar. Events carry `u` (the slot), projectile ids `t:s:u:slot`; `eventLines` heads the log with `|doubles`. People: `personInput` moves the controlled slot (`leadOf`: `ctl`, or the other while that one is vacant) with the input and drives the partner with `think(b, unit, {lead})` (`partnerInput`, stubbed in tests); a switch goes to a slot waiting for a Pokémon (the controlled one first), Mega Evolution to whichever slot can. Input rows: `[tick, side, mx, my, move, roll, switch, focus, plus, mega, ctl, tgt]` in doubles; rows without the last two read as 0. `view()` adds `doubles`, per side `ctl`, `tgt`, `units` (each slot's place, act, `aim`, `follow`, `helped`, `vacant`) and per Pokémon `field` (its slot or -1); the side's own fields are those of the controlled slot.
- `brain.mjs` — the AI's reflexes: reaction time, dodge chance and switching by level; move scores (expected damage, setup, status, protection, recovery, hazards) weighted by style; spacing for melee or ranged moves; picking the next Pokémon by matchup. Version 2: it Mega Evolves as soon as it can, arms Plus on its strongest ready move when a segment is full and the foe is in reach (keeping the gauge while its Pokémon can still Mega Evolve), and on rocks it steers around them, steps out of a blocked line and scores ranged moves into a rock down. Doubles: `chooseAim` locks on the foe it can hurt most and that threatens it or its partner most (a person's partner keeps the person's locked foe unless the other scores 1.5× + 0.2 more), `spreadValue` adds the second foe caught by a spread move at ×0.75 and takes off what a quake would do to a partner nearby, `danger` also watches both foes and the partner's quake wind-ups, movement keeps 4.2 m from the partner and a person's partner within 7.5 m of the person's Pokémon; the partner leaves switching, Plus and Mega Evolution to the person and scores Helping Hand and Follow Me.
- Engine commands: `rt_check` (validate a spec, first view), `rt_team` (a team's real stats and cooldowns), `rt_cooldowns`, `rt_play_out` (AI vs AI), `rt_replay` (inputs, or a forfeit at a tick). `stats.mjs` plays series and tournaments in either mode.
- App: battles carry `mode` and `replay` ({time_limit, inputs, tick, version, arena}; new battles store version 3 (`RT_RULES_VERSION`; `_rt_format` keeps singles and doubles formats and turns random ones into the custom game of their generation and kind) and the arena from `rt_arena`, default `rocks`; rows without them replay as version 1 on an open arena); a live real-time battle saves its inputs every 5 s (`battle_rt_save`) and ends with `battle_rt_finish`, which replays them in the engine and stores that result; watched ones run `rt_play_out` as a background job while the screen replays the same battle. Runs (gym and league challenges) keep their mode and arena for every stage; series and tournaments pass the arena and version to every battle they store.
- `pmd.py` — downloads SpriteCollab's `AnimData.xml` and sheets of a Pokémon (forms through the tracker, shiny under `0000/0001`), works out each animation's ground anchor from its `-Shadow.png`, caches meta and sheets; routes `/api/sprites/pmd/{dex}` and `/api/sprites/pmd-sheet/{key}/{anim}`; starting a real-time battle prefetches both teams.
- Client, doubles: `input.js` keeps `ctl` / `tgt` per person (Tab / C and X, gamepad L or the left stick click in the simple layout and the right stick click; two people C / X and ; / '), `render.js` draws every slot (keys `side * 2 + slot` for flashes, labels and fades), each HP bar with its slot letter, a chevron over the controlled Pokémon, a reticle on the locked foe (turning while the lock-on is held), an orange ring under a Follow Me user, and `vfx.js` places effects on the event's slot. `RtScreen.jsx` shows both Pokémon of each side in the bars, a control strip (controlled Pokémon, locked foe, swap and next-foe buttons), both actives in the party strip and the slot waiting for a Pokémon.
- Client: `RtScreen.jsx` (loop, keyboard and gamepad through `input.js` with the Z-A layout by default (Shift / ZL lock-on drawn as a reticle, moves in an X A B Y diamond, Space / Y roll, F / ZL + + Plus, M / R Mega, Q / E / R and the D-pad for the party, + pause) or the simple one (always locked on; L Mega, − Plus), HUD with each trainer's gauge, Plus and Mega buttons, pause and forfeit, result, replay with speeds), `render.js` (canvas: court with its rocks, trainers, depth-sorted sprites with `pmd.js`'s animator (Mega formes with their own sprites, the base one when there is none), a facing arrow when not locked on, telegraphs, floating numbers, screen shake), `vfx.js` (the look of each move, drawing only: rays from the user to the beam's head by move, shaped projectiles and trails, melee marks by move name, drops from the sky, bursts and quakes by move, hit particles by type, boost and heal auras, status particles, weather, pink sparks and echoing rings for Plus moves, a burst of light for Mega Evolution), `ModeFields.jsx` (the mode switch and the arena in battles, challenges, series and tournaments), `CooldownsTab.jsx` (the trainer page's real-time tab). Words in `rt/strings.js`, merged into the app's dictionary.
