# Menu and battle presentation

The October 2026 visual pass preserves navigation, actions, game rules, saved inputs, teams and results. `client/src/arcade.css` gives the existing surfaces a slate-and-silver cabinet treatment with warm gold selection, cyan and coral fighter corners, squared controls and self-hosted Barlow Condensed display lettering. Body copy keeps the inherited readable sans-serif.

Home actions use the already-catalogued Platinum menu sprites at a larger size. Battle setup adds a large preview of each selected trainer and its existing species list; saved and game character selection, full-team choices, AI, formats and rules remain in their existing controls. Image failures keep the existing initial fallback. Menus retain all nineteen navigation destinations, native disclosures and focus indicators. Small screens retain the scrollable navigation strip.

Classic and real-time HUDs share the new presentation. The RT VS intro adds clipped corner streaks and uses the display font. Its timing, trainer image fallback and simulation hold are unchanged. `ImpactAccents` adds a contact ring and eight rays on a critical, super-effective or finishing hit, using the hit type and actual target coordinates. Ordinary hits get a smaller ring. Zero damage and subsequent multihit events are excluded; at most sixteen marks exist at once. They use `Show.update` presentation time, so pause holds them and a fresh replay renderer clears them. Reduced motion uses a stationary ring and no rays or moving intro streaks. The pre-existing animation toggles and supplied attack atlases remain available.

The app and freshly exported playable pages share the stylesheet, font and renderer. The font embeds into portable HTML; old exports must be exported again. Existing source-art credit remains in the supplied-sprite manifest and Credits. Barlow Condensed is by Jeremy Tribby, under SIL Open Font License 1.1; the unmodified font and license come from `google/fonts`, `ofl/barlowcondensed`. No new Pokémon artwork or externally hosted runtime dependency is introduced.

This is presentation, not a new moveset or a claim of bespoke choreography for every attack. The existing mass renderer keeps its own battle choreography; this pass's new contact rings belong to ordinary RT battles.

## Body acting and damage feedback

Ordinary RT battles now connect preparation, release and recovery around the source sprite. `FighterReactions` reads the public view/events, keeps four fixed presentation slots and samples the renderer clock. Windups brace backwards; releases give melee a stronger impulse than ranged attacks. Positive hits recoil away from an unambiguous releasing unit, with stronger compression on larger/critical damage; ambiguous matching releases in doubles compress in place. Protection braces briefly; a genuine miss gives the aimed defender a sidestep (fog misses do not); healing gives a small upward recovery. Same-frame multi-hits merge into the strongest bounded recoil. Recall/switch and replay reset clear the relevant acting, and slot identity prevents a replacement inheriting it.

These transforms wrap PMD, imported walking and static fallback art. Fallbacks also share the cached white hit silhouette. Ground rings, collision/targeting coordinates, HP bars and all input logs remain at their actual locations. Dig, Fly, Bounce, rolling moves and Seismic Toss retain their existing special choreography. Faint poses retain the existing fade. No new artwork, animation library, simulation event or gameplay timing is introduced.

Damage numbers use the bundled condensed face, a brief impact-scale accent, a short rise and a late fade; same-tick multi-hit damage is totalled into one figure with a hit count (for example 35% x5). Pause freezes both body acting and numbers; speed changes follow the presentation delta and seeking creates a clean owner. Reduced motion removes added displacement, rotation, squash and number travel/scale, while the existing hit flash, figures and outcome labels remain visible. New portable exports and recorded ordinary RT replays use the same renderer. Classic and mass battles retain their own choreography; this addition does not claim new reactions for those renderers.


## Dynamax and Z staging

`SpecialPresentation` adds event-driven Dynamax gathering pillars, uniform foot-anchored growth, an expanding ground shockwave, three persistent storm clouds and an on-field timeout contraction. Z windups concentrate typed energy into a crystal; release retains the supplied localized move-title art. Special projectile heads follow actual projectile coordinates, with electric forks, flame tongues, crystal shards, spiral currents or contact cuts according to type. A confirmed first hit adds a larger typed impact; misses, immunity and protection never fabricate one. Status specials retain their charge and release feedback without a damage impact. A short trainer command cut-in occupies the opposite corner in landscape and the free middle band in portrait.

The same renderer ships in the app, recorded ordinary RT battles and newly exported HTML. No simulation timing, input protocol, move rules or Pokémon pixels change. Animation state uses the existing presentation clock, freezes on pause and resets on seek; the crystal follows the actual windup rather than expiring by wall time. Transients are bounded, and doubles owners include side/unit/party slot. Reduced motion keeps the giant state, still clouds/crystal and fading outcome marks without growth, orbiting streaks or expanding attack accents. Plain presentation retains state indicators and omits the added cinematic layers. This is typed choreography, not a separately authored animation for every signature move or a claim of measured competitor parity.
